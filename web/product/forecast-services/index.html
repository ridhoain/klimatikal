Which forecast fits your decision?

Many activities and assets depend on the weather and climate, and decisions must be made to keep them running into the future. Many meteorological forecasts and services exist, but finding the one that fits a given need is not always easy. Use this guide to find a suitable forecast service and to develop your use case.

How to read this guide
  • Position. The default view is two-dimensional: planning horizon across and spatial extent upward, both logarithmic, with items numbered along the diagonal from small and short-range to large and long-range. Selecting an impact variable filters the items that forecast it and opens the three-dimensional view, where each sphere is also stretched to the impact proximity that approach typically covers.
  • Symbols. Each item has a weather symbol, coloured by the main variable group it forecasts. Sphere colour still shows maturity or skill evidence. Numbers are shown in the list and on hover.
  • Variables, use cases and products. Each entry lists the variables the approach can forecast, examples of use by other institutions or companies, and existing products where one is available. Examples are described as the sources report them; vendor and research examples are labelled as such.
  • Placement and maturity. These are Klimatikal's assessments as of 14 November 2026 and will change as evidence does. Established means routine in operational use; experimental means early, limited or still being verified.
  • Skill evidence. The tags show where the cited skill was measured: Indonesia or Southeast Asia, other tropical regions, mid-latitude regions, or global studies. An item can carry several. Items with no figure are marked either as having none found or as not verifiable, because they are projections or loss models. Figures are quoted from the cited sources for the metric, region and period each used. They are not comparable across approaches. Where a source is a poster, preprint or vendor material, the entry says so, and where nothing was found for Indonesia, it says that too.
  • Scope. This is a guide to choosing an approach, not a forecast, and not a recommendation for any specific decision.
